Tomato Risotto with Frizzled Red Onions
Servings: 4, more as a side Time: 50 minutes Source: Smitten Kitchen
2 pounds (905 grams) large, ripe beefsteak tomatoes, halved
1 medium-large red onion, halved and thinly sliced
Olive oil
3 garlic cloves, minced
1/3 cup (80 ml) dry white wine
1 cup (200 grams) uncooked arborio or carnaroli, or another short-grained rice, such as sushi rice
Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
Red pepper flakes, to taste
About 2 cups (275 ml) water
2 to 3 tablespoons (30 to 45 grams) butter or olive oil, to finish
Grated parmesan cheese, to finish
Handful basil leaves, to finish
Heat oven: To 350°F.
Prepare the risotto: Grate halved tomatoes, the cut side pressing against the large holes of a box grater, pressing the tomato flesh and juices through and reserving as much as you can of the outer skin. Discard it. You should have about 2 1/2 to 3 cups of tomato pulp.

In a 4-quart Dutch oven [I’m using this] or deep, oven-safe saucepan with a lid, heat 3 to 4 tablespoons olive oil on medium-high heat. Add red onion and cook, stirring, until it softens, then continue to cook until the pieces become deeply golden brown and mostly crisp, about 8 to 10 minutes. Transfer half to a paper towel-lined plate and season with salt. Set it aside.

Add garlic to onions left in the pan and cook, stirring, for one minute. Season with salt and freshly ground black pepper, plus a pinch or two of red pepper flakes. Add uncooked rice and toast gently in the onion and garlic for 3 minutes. Add wine and cook, stirring, until it disappears. Pour in your grated tomato pulp, then 2 cups of water. Season it well — I add 2 teaspoons kosher salt to start, then more to taste, plus many grinds of black pepper. Bring mixture to a simmer, then turn heat off.

Place lid on pot and transfer to the oven. Bake risotto for 25 to 30 minutes, or until most of the liquid is absorbed, but it looks a tiny bit watery. I like to check in halfway just to make sure all of the liquid isn’t absorbed. If it looks low, add another 1/2 cup water.

To finish: Transfer pan to a trivet or cooling rack on your counter. Remove the lid and stir mixture for 2 minutes, or until it looks more creamy and risotto-like. Taste and adjust seasonings, adding more salt and pepper to your taste. Add 2 to 3 tablespoons of butter (or olive oil) to finish, stirring it in. Scatter with toppings of your choice, such as parmesan and torn basil. Finish with reserved frizzled onions and serve.
